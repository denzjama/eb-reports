*A distinctive, well-evidenced promise of working alongside Claude every day, weakened by two contradictions inside the job ads and by proof that sits off the careers path.*

**Employer Brand Score — 76 out of 100** · coverage 100% · high reliability

- The promise to work alongside Claude every day is the most distinctive and best-supported part of the brand. Two of Anthropic's own workforce reports back it with survey and usage data from 2025 and 2026, but neither is linked from the careers page or from any of the seven ads read.
- Two promises are contradicted inside the job ads. The careers page says what you can do matters more than where you learned it, while the Pre-training ad lists an MS or PhD as a qualification. Every ad offers flexible working hours, while the Sydney support ad sets fixed 12pm to 8pm hours with on-call duty.
- Decision facts are strong for US roles: pay ranges in five of seven ads, specific benefits and a frank FAQ. They thin out elsewhere: the Tokyo and Sydney ads give no pay, and the benefits list does not say which countries it covers.
- All seven ads end with the same block about research being big science, including the sales, customer success and support roles.
- The outside view is missing. No review text could be read, and 1 of 15 YouTube videos shows a team at work, with none linking to jobs.

## What works

**A promise only Anthropic can make, with data behind it.** The AI guidance says Anthropic uses Claude every day and looks for people who collaborate well with AI. Anthropic's December 2025 report shows the 132 engineers and researchers surveyed in August 2025 self-reporting Claude use in 59% of their work. The October 2026 report says that by August, agents in employee-directed sessions worked six times as many hours as the people directing them. Two reports a year apart make this a current practice, not a one-off.

**Candid employee voice that names downsides.** The workforce reports quote employees on what got worse as well as better: some worry about skills atrophying, a senior engineer says junior colleagues come with questions less often, and one employee describes coming to work to put themselves out of a job. The reports also list their own limits, such as non-anonymous responses.

**Hiring rules stated plainly.** The AI guidance says when Claude is allowed at each stage and how Anthropic uses Claude in hiring, and states that Claude does not make hiring decisions and candidate data is not used for training. The FAQ says there is no feedback on applications, re-applying is possible after 12 months, and candidates can take their time on an offer.

**Pay and benefits in figures.** Five of seven ads give an annual range and explain that sales ranges are on-target earnings. The careers page lists 22 weeks of paid parental leave, a $500 a month wellness and time-saver stipend, and 1:1 equity donation matching up to 25% of the equity grant.

## The main problems

**Two promises on the careers page are contradicted by the job ads.** The credentials promise is one of four core claims and has otherwise good support. A flagship research ad that lists an advanced degree, with a lower minimum further down the same page, leaves the actual requirement unclear. The flexible-hours line sits in an ad that fixes the hours.

> Careers page: "We care about what you can do, not where you learned to do it."

**The strongest proof is kept away from the careers path, while the culture section relies on principle statements.** The careers page carries promises; the evidence for them sits in research posts a candidate has to find separately. The culture claim has only one weak supporting passage in the material, and it is in a report, not beside the principle.

> Careers page principle: "Anthropic is a high-trust, low-ego organization." No example of how this works sits next to it.

**Sales, customer success and support ads close with a block written for researchers.** The role content in these ads is well adapted, but their only section on what makes Anthropic different talks about research teams. The careers page already says non-technical teams are small and shape how the company works; that idea does not reach these ads.

> All seven ads, under How we're different: "We believe that the highest-impact AI research will be big science."

**Decision facts are thinner for roles outside the US.** A candidate in Tokyo or Sydney cannot assess the reward side of the offer from the ad, and cannot tell whether the benefit figures on the careers page apply to them.

> The Tokyo Applied AI Architect and Sydney Product Support ads show no pay range; the other five ads do.

## Where to start

1. Fix the two contradictions: decide whether the Pre-training degree is required, and drop or explain flexible hours in shift-based ads.
2. Link the two workforce reports and the take-home post from the careers page and the AI guidance, with two or three dated figures next to the promise.
3. Give commercial and support ads their own closing block, and add pay and country-specific benefits to non-US ads.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

*Microsoft's job ads and programme pages give specific, checkable detail: pay ranges, in-office days, rotation lengths. The pages that state the offer lead with the mission and “growth mindset”, which a comparable employer could reuse unchanged, and back them with customer figures instead of the proof published elsewhere on the site.*

**Employer Brand Score — 69 out of 100** · coverage 100% · high reliability

- The strongest material is operational. All four ads state a base pay range with level and country, and the in-office days. The hiring page gives the number and length of interviews and says plainly that applicants who are not selected usually get no individual feedback.
- The pages that state the offer lead with the mission and “growth mindset”. Neither passes the substitution test, and the culture page backs customer focus with Fortune 500 and cloud-region figures that say nothing about the job.
- Promise and proof sit on different pages. Growth is promised to “anyone”, but every growth mechanism shown is for people starting their careers. Six of eight promises are partly supported; none is fully supported.
- Employee voice comes from a diversity storytelling hub. The two text stories found date from 2023, and none comes from the datacenter or rotation hiring lines the site promotes.
- On Comparably, benefits draw praise, while pay, recognition and senior leadership are in tension with the promise that rewards reflect impact. The sample is one platform with undated answers, and reviews do not enter the score.

## What works

**Pay ranges in every ad reviewed.** All four captured ads state the typical base pay range with the job level and country: €44,000 to €57,200 for the Dublin sales role (IC2); separate ranges for Ireland, Romania and Czechia for the recruiter role (IC4); USD $70,500 to $139,700 across the U.S. for customer success, with a separate $95,000 to $153,200 range for the San Francisco Bay area and New York City; CAD $43,800 to $50,000 for the Toronto internship. This is direct evidence for the benefits page's promise of transparency from the first conversation about pay.

**In-office days stated before anyone applies.** The benefits page explains a work-site field that runs from 0 days a week in the office to fully on-site, and all four ads carry it: 3 days a week for both Dublin roles, 0 days (remote) for customer success, fully on-site for the datacenter internship. The Dublin sales ad adds that the role allows up to 50% work from home and that relocation may be required.

**A hiring process described with numbers.** The hiring page says most interviews include 2 to 4 conversations of up to an hour each, that a resume uploaded for job recommendations is purged within 24 hours, that the match indicator does not guarantee any outcome, and that applicants not selected for a screen or interview usually get no individual feedback. Early-career applicants learn they will have an initial interview and, if selected, a final one.

**Early-career programmes with visible structure.** The early-in-profession page gives each programme a length and shape: the Finance Rotation Program is two years of four six-month rotations, SCORE is three one-year rotations, the HR rotation runs three years, and the Discovery Program is a four-week internship for rising first-year students in Redmond or Atlanta. Two ads add what EiP participants get: immersive training, structured job shadowing and continuous support from dedicated mentors, with heavy investment in the first six months.

## The main problems

**The offer leads with the company's mission, and neither the mission nor “growth mindset” says what working here gives you.** The home page says the mission sets Microsoft apart but shows nothing about how it shapes a job. The culture page names four attributes, and each reads as true for a comparable employer once the name is removed. The material that could carry the offer (pay ranges, the work-site field, programme structures) appears only deeper in the site.

> Home page: “When you join Microsoft, you’re not just stepping into a job— you’re stepping into a mission-driven company where purpose is at the heart of everything we do.”

**Growth is promised to everyone, but the evidence covers only people starting their careers.** The material shows no mechanism, path or figure for someone joining mid-career or growing after the first role. The promise reaches further than its evidence, and growth is the most repeated promise after the mission.

> Culture page: “We know that anyone can evolve their thinking, expand their skills, and grow their career because we’ve built the systems and support to make that happen.”

**Customer focus and the mission are backed by company figures that say nothing about the job.** The figures describe customers and infrastructure. The culture page does not say what being customer obsessed means in an employee's week, although the customer success ad describes exactly that for one team. The comparison “more than any other provider” has no source on the page.

> Culture page, under the customer-obsessed attribute: “That’s why 95% of Fortune 500 companies trust Microsoft, and why our cloud spans 60+ regions worldwide, more than any other provider.”

**Benefits are promised everywhere and described only for the US.** The benefits page lists categories (health, saving, time away, family leave, education, discounts) without amounts or conditions, and the detail sits in a US-only section. Three of the four captured ads are outside the US. “World-class” is not backed by any comparison in the material.

> Benefits page: “No matter where you are or what role you’re in, the support to grow, the space to learn, and the care to thrive are always within reach.”

## Where to start

1. Rewrite the home and culture pages to lead with facts already published: in-office days in every posting, EiP onboarding, donation matching.
2. Show how growth works after the first role, or limit the growth promise to the programmes that support it.
3. Collect recent, dated stories from the EiP, rotation and datacenter hiring lines and link them from those pages.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Pay and pay rises** (12 / 75): Eight of the 12 are critical: shrinking bonus and stock budgets, rises below inflation, pay that does not follow performance. Three are positive about pay overall.
- **Benefits** (8 / 75): Health insurance, parental leave, the pension plan and office perks are named positively. Several answers call benefits the best part of the package.
- **Senior leadership and management** (14 / 75): Four answers praise leaders for being accessible or for giving ownership. Nine criticise senior leadership for not caring about staff, morale or retention. Some answers separate a supportive direct manager from the levels above.
- **Colleagues and team** (15 / 75): Ten answers praise colleagues and team spirit. Five ask for better treatment of new joiners, less focus on individual credit, or enough people to do the work.
- **Recognition and fairness** (5 / 75): Five answers say recognition, promotion or pay follow popularity or favourites. These overlap with the pay theme.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

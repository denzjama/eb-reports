*H-E-B's careers site has real specifics, from dated career journeys to a frank description of warehouse work, but it leads with lines any large retailer could use and never explains its most distinctive promise: that Partners are owners.*

**Employer Brand Score — 57 out of 100** · coverage 94% · high reliability

- The lead lines fit any large retailer: many careers, endless opportunity, every person counts. The one idea in this material that is close to being only H-E-B's, that Partners are owners in the company, appears twice in passing and is explained nowhere.
- Career growth is the only promise with meaningful support: named, dated career journeys and a programme figure (96% of ATL participants promoted to Team Leader upon successful completion). All four storytellers read hold leadership roles, and no hourly Partner speaks.
- The hiring-process page contradicts itself on whether the video interview is required, three of its tips tiles show placeholder text, and Partner counts differ across pages (160,000+, 170,000+, 175,000+).
- The most concrete benefit for hourly staff on the site, viewing hours, swapping shifts and requesting time off by phone, sits in a digital blog post framed around 2019, not on the store page.
- On Comparably, 70 undated answers mix praise for benefits, teams and respect with complaints about managers and pay. Three say part-time Partners miss out, one of them on the stock program, which bears directly on the unexplained ownership line.

## What works

**The warehouse and transport page names the hard parts.** The supply-chain page says the work involves heavy lifting, selecting and packing, a rapid pace, hot areas near equipment, cold areas in walk-in coolers and freezers, and frequent wet conditions from cleaning and sanitizing. It is the only careers page read that says who the work suits, which makes it the model for the other pages.

**Career journeys with names, roles and dates.** Luis Martinez's story traces his path from Receiving Specialist at Joe V's Smart Shop to Floor Manager in 2012, a SORL graduate in 2018, Talent Manager in 2022, Store Team Leader in 2023 and Top Store Team Leader in March 2025. Justin Tippett's runs from store operations through five years leading the Continuous Improvement & Design team, Procurement and HR Director for San Antonio to leading the Joe V's division. Both name their mentors and the moments they hesitated.

**Leadership programmes with a stated outcome.** The Students page describes SORL as a store leadership rotational programme that, upon successful completion, gives the skills to become an Assistant Store Director, and states that 96% of ATL participants are promoted to Team Leader upon successful completion. The internship timeline gives months for applications, interviews, offers and the 10-week programme.

**A hiring process explained down to the error messages.** The Candidate Toolkit lays out four steps. The FAQs cover scams (recruiters contact candidates from H-E-B email addresses and never request payment), age limits by role, answering the video interview in a native language or ASL where applicable, reusing an interview with identical questions completed in the past 30 days, and checking application status.

## The main problems

**The most distinctive promise, that Partners are owners, is stated twice and explained nowhere.** Of the four lead claims, this one comes closest to being true only of H-E-B, and it is the only one with no support at all on the pages read. The sentence is also unqualified: it presents Partners as owners without any condition. On Comparably, three of the 70 answers say part-time Partners miss out on benefits or hours, and one says part-time Partners cannot qualify for the stock program. That is one reviewer's account, not a fact, but it makes eligibility the obvious question for the page to answer.

> “Regularly named a top employer and retailer, H-E-B's more than 160,000 Partners (employees), who are owners in our company, are at the heart of what makes our business successful and great.” (Our Careers page)

**The lead messages fit any large retailer: many careers, endless opportunity, every person counts.** Swap the name for another grocer and each of these lines still reads true: none contains a figure, a place or a practice. The material that would make them H-E-B's own already exists elsewhere on the site. The ATL figure and the SORL path sit on the Students page, and the career journeys sit in the blog. The top-employer line names no ranking, so it claims more than the page shows.

> Home page headline ONE COMPANY, MANY CAREERS, followed by: “At H-E-B, a job is more than just a job - it's an opportunity for a rewarding career that allows you to use your unique talents and passions to innovate and make a difference across multiple areas of the business.”

**The hiring-process page contradicts itself on whether the video interview is required.** The two statements cannot both be true. A candidate who receives no invitation cannot tell from the page whether something went wrong or the role does not use HireVue. The page does not say which rule applies, and the audit does not assume one.

> Step 2: “After you apply, for some of our roles, you may receive an email inviting you to complete an online interview (HireVue).” and “If the role you are applying to does not require a HireVue, a recruiter will be reviewing your application and will conduct initial interviews if you are selected.”

**The store page shows no work, and the clearest benefit for hourly staff is buried in an old tech blog post.** Store roles are the only ones the site opens from age 16, yet the store page says nothing about tasks, shifts or what each area involves. The supply-chain page, by contrast, names the lifting, the hot and cold areas and the pace. Shift tools are the kind of fact the store page lacks, but it is unclear whether the post's description and usage figures still hold today.

> Store page: “It takes hundreds of H-E-B Partners in each store to serve our communities. Whether you’re in search of a Part-time job or your next career, we have aisles and aisles of opportunities!” It is followed by a list of 12 areas and an FAQ on age, experience and the video interview.

## Where to start

1. Explain the ownership promise where it is made: what Partners own, who qualifies and what it has meant, in figures.
2. Make the hiring-process page state one video-interview rule, replace the placeholder tiles and date the headcount figures.
3. Rebuild the store careers page around the work, the path up and an hourly Partner's own story.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Pay and benefits** (14 / 70): Six answers praise benefits and pay. Eight raise low pay, unequal pay for the same job, or store pay compared with executives.
- **Part-time hours and eligibility** (3 / 70): One answer says part-time Partners cannot qualify for the stock program; two ask for more hours and benefits for part-timers. Whether eligibility rules exist is a question for H-E-B.
- **Management and leadership** (18 / 70): Six answers praise leaders' availability and support. Twelve ask for better-trained managers, less favouritism and more respect.
- **Coworkers and team** (11 / 70): Four answers praise mutual help. Seven mention gossip, favouritism or low effort; most of these answer a prompt that asks what coworkers should improve.
- **Feeling valued and culture** (9 / 70): Answers mention friendliness, respect, fair treatment and colleagues from different countries. One calls the experience horrible; one asks for kinder treatment after mistakes.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

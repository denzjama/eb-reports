*Google's public hiring copy is concrete where it describes a single role and its pay, and generic where it explains why to work there. The idea repeated everywhere, helping billions of users, is a product fact that never says what it means for the person doing the job.*

**Employer Brand Score — 64 out of 100** · coverage 99% · high reliability

- Job ads are the strongest material. Five of seven state a pay range with bonus target and equity, six of seven define the seniority level in plain words, and the DeepMind and Search ads describe the actual project.
- The proposition rests on reach. Helping billions of users appears in the careers summaries, five of seven ads, the intern story and two videos. It is consistent and generic: outside the DeepMind ad, no touchpoint says what that reach means for the person in the role.
- Career growth is the weakest promise. Ads mention switching teams and varied career pathways without saying how. The YouTube career stories describe routes into Google. Two of 75 Comparably answers say promotion criteria are unclear.
- The How we hire summary promises every candidate the same information. The interview tips found sit on YouTube, cover sales and AI/ML only, and are not linked from the ads read. Three of five Comparably answers on improving interviews mention length or poor communication.
- The data center technician ad is the most candid text in the set: heavy lifting, nights, weekends, holidays, fully on site, no visa sponsorship. It still does not say how often those shifts come up or how they are paid.

## What works

**Pay is stated with its structure.** Five of seven ads give a base range plus bonus target and equity. The data center technician in West Memphis: US $105,000 to $145,000 with a 15% bonus target. The account executive in Hamburg: €78,000 to €79,500 with a 35% bonus target. The DeepMind product manager: US $138,000 to $197,000. Each says individual pay depends on job-related skills, experience and relevant education or training.

**Some ads show the actual project.** The DeepMind ad says Deep Zoom has launched its first 20 curated worlds in the Gemini app and the role drives expansion to thousands of topic experiences, with quality owned across storytelling, factual accuracy and visual fidelity. The Bengaluru ad ties the role to Search Intelligence features for the Indian market. These are the most specific statements about working at Google in the set.

**The data center ad states its demands.** It lists lifting 50lb (23kg), long periods of physical exertion, weekends, night shifts, holidays and shift schedules, travel, 100% on-site work and no U.S. immigration sponsorship, and describes a typical day of moving and installing racks.

**Seniority is defined, not just labelled.** Six of seven ads give a level with a one-line definition. Early, for example, is defined as experience completing work as directed and collaborating with teammates.

## The main problems

**The central idea is product reach, and almost nothing says what it means for the person in the role.** The figure describes Google's products. Outside the DeepMind ad, the material does not connect it to what a person in a given role owns, decides or changes. The most repeated message in the set therefore carries no information about the job.

> Careers home summary: “Make a global impact with innovative technology that helps billions of users.”

**Career growth is promised without a mechanism.** No touchpoint says how a team move works, how often people make one, or what promotion requires. Growth is offered as a reason to join in two ads and a video series, and it is the least supported promise in the set.

> Bengaluru ad: “As a software engineer, you will work on a specific project critical to Google’s needs with opportunities to switch teams and projects as you and our fast-paced business grow and evolve.”

**The benefits promise is a superlative with nothing behind it in the captured text.** World-class is a comparison the material gives no basis for, and the sentence has a grammatical slip. The benefits page body could not be read, so its full content is unknown; the summary line itself names no benefit.

> Benefits page summary: “We strive to provide Googlers and their loved ones with a world-class benefits.”

**Ad openings are built from superlatives and stock phrases.** These paragraphs come before the role and make comparative claims (unparalleled, more than anyone, the industry's best) with no comparison in the material. The two sales ads open with an almost identical paragraph, so it says nothing about either specific role.

> Hamburg and Sydney sales ads: “Businesses of all shapes and sizes rely on Google’s unparalleled advertising solutions to help them grow in today's dynamic marketing environment.”

## Where to start

1. Rewrite the shared opening paragraph of each ad family and the Working at Google page so the reach line ends in what this role owns.
2. Publish how team moves and promotion work, with a figure and a period, and link it from every ad that promises growth.
3. Replace the benefits superlative with the main benefits and their terms, and add pay ranges to ads that lack them.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Hiring process** (3 / 75): Three of the five answers on improving the interview process mention its length or slow, unclear communication. The answers on what people liked about interviewing are mostly stock praise.
- **Management and feedback** (12 / 75): Three answers ask leadership not to retaliate against people who give feedback. Others mention bureaucracy, corporate language, strategy and empathy. Two answers praise clear instructions or progressive management.
- **Growth and promotion** (2 / 75): One answer says management cannot explain what promotion requires. Another says the company misrepresents how its rating system works. Two answers are too few to show a pattern.
- **Pay and recognition** (3 / 75): Answers say recognition lags when workload rises, and pay does not keep up with living costs. The question itself asks why people feel undervalued, so the tone follows the question.
- **Pace and workload** (6 / 75): Reviewers describe a fast pace and quick decisions; some value it, others link it to rising workload and less personal connection.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

*IKEA's careers material shows a specific, Swedish-rooted way of working and gives hourly candidates real decision facts, but it leads with a customer mission and promises career mobility without showing how mobility works.*

**Employer Brand Score — 66 out of 100** · coverage 100% · high reliability

- The two hourly job ads are the strongest material. The Florida restaurant ad states the hourly pay range, weekly hours, contract type and required availability. The Aalborg warehouse ad states the exact shift times, the physical demands and a named contact.
- The first thing a candidate reads is a customer mission: make millions of people look forward to going home. What working at IKEA offers has to be pieced together from sub-pages.
- The main career promise, that you can stay and go almost anywhere, rests on a list of IKEA business units. No route, rule, example or number shows how moves happen, and the Aalborg ad repeats the promise as endless possibilities to grow.
- The informal, Swedish-rooted way of working (first names, casual dress, Fika, talking to anyone regardless of title) is the one claim tied to IKEA alone. None of the three ads mentions these practices. The Amsterdam ad brings in the value of togetherness only to explain why engineers must spend most of their time in the office.
- The most specific tech proof sits in the Amsterdam ad (named product, stack, partner team), while the Data & Technology page relies on brightest minds and the forefront of technology. The material does not say whether the ad's team belongs to IKEA Data & Technology. On YouTube, 1 of the 15 latest videos is about work.

## What works

**The hourly ads give the facts needed to decide.** The Florida restaurant ad states a pay range of $17.58 to $24.83 an hour, 34 to 40 hours a week, a permanent full-time contract and that weekend and evening availability is required. It lists benefits with their conditions: medical plans need at least 20 hours a week, paid parental leave is up to 16 weeks. The Aalborg ad states an average of 10 hours a week, weekday mornings from 05:00 to 10:00 plus every second Saturday, and that co-workers can give input to the schedule.

**The warehouse ad names the hard parts and how they are handled.** The Aalborg ad asks for good physical shape and the ability to handle hard manual labour. It explains why the pace is high: the shelves must be filled before the store opens at 10 AM. It then says how the team copes, through mutual support and the training the employer commits to, and gives a named team leader's email for questions.

**The way of working is shown through practices.** The working-together pages describe first-name terms, casual dress, open offices, the freedom to talk to any co-worker regardless of title, and Fika as a daily coffee break that IKEA locations have kept for over 70 years. These are linked to the Småland origin and the value of Simplicity. This is the one proposition in the material that a comparable employer could not reuse unchanged.

**The hiring process is described and open.** Prepare for success lays out four steps from pre-screening to onboarding, says the offer comes in writing and invites candidates to ask for adjustments at any stage. It and the Aalborg ad both encourage people to apply without meeting every qualification.

## The main problems

**The headline promise is about customers, not about working at IKEA.** The first screen tells a candidate what IKEA does for customers. It says nothing about what the job gives the person doing it. The offer to employees appears only on sub-pages, so the line repeated across three touchpoints carries no employee benefit.

> "Make millions of people look forward to going home." (jobs.ikea.com home page, first screen, followed by headcount, country and store figures)

**The mobility promise has no mechanism and no proof.** The only support is a list of where IKEA operates (stores, development centres, factories, distribution facilities, studios, office hubs) and one employee's sentence that there are opportunities to take on more responsibility. Nothing says who can move, how to apply internally, or how many people have moved. The promise is the careers site's main reason to join, and it is repeated in an ad for a role averaging 10 hours a week without any route attached.

> "You can stay with us – and go almost anywhere" (page title, jobs.ikea.com)

**The tech page uses stock phrases while its best proof sits in a single ad.** The page names no product, problem or tool. The Amsterdam ad names a product, a stack and a partner team, but it describes Group Digital and Ingka Digital, and the material does not say whether these belong to IKEA Data & Technology. Until that is clear, the ad's detail cannot be read as evidence for the page.

> "To do that, we're looking for the brightest minds in tech to join us!" (Data & Technology page)

**Values are offered as what makes IKEA different, but most are shown only in words.** Simplicity is demonstrated through Fika and first names. The other seven values, including togetherness and give and take responsibility, are defined in belief statements without a practice or example. The Life at IKEA section headed Putting words into action contains no action.

> "Our shared values and strong culture make IKEA unique." (Our values page)

## Where to start

1. Add one line about working at IKEA under the headline on jobs.ikea.com, built from what the culture pages already say.
2. Back the mobility promise with how internal moves work and real figures and routes, then carry it into the ads.
3. Rebuild the Data & Technology page around named products and team set-ups, and replace the one-line quotes with real stories.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

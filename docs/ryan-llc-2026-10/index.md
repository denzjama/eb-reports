*Ryan’s job ads give candidates a concrete benefits list and a clear picture of the tasks. The employer story wrapped around them is one shared paragraph of stock phrases and superlatives, and its promise of control over your own time sits in the same ad as a 50+ hour standard workweek.*

**Employer Brand Score — 57 out of 100** · coverage 97% · medium reliability

- The ads do two things well. Four US ads carry the same concrete benefits list, and every ad lists real tasks and tools; every experienced-hire ad gives the share of time spent travelling.
- The employer story around them is one paragraph repeated on the job board and in every ad. It is built from stock phrases and ends in an unsupported superlative. Of the seven promises the audit found, only the benefits package and hands-on internship work have meaningful support.
- The promise that employees get ownership of their time sits in the Principal ad next to a 50+ hour standard workweek and 30 to 50% travel. The ad does not say how the two fit.
- Recognition is claimed but not shown. The ads say “Award-Winning Culture” and name no award. The independent Great Place To Work profile lists a Fortune 100 Best Companies to Work For rank for every year from 2018 to 2026 and a 95% survey result. None of this reaches the ads.
- In 38 Comparably answers, colleagues, flexibility and the hiring process come up positively. Six answers describe the hiring process, while the ads explain no hiring steps.

## What works

**A concrete benefits list, identical across the US ads.** All four experienced-hire ads open with the same Why Ryan? list. It gives terms, not adjectives: 14 weeks of 100% paid leave for new parents with adoption included, benefits eligibility from day one, tuition reimbursement after one year of service, a fertility assistance program, and a four-week company-paid sabbatical after five years of service. A candidate can check each item against another offer.

**Job ads show the actual work.** Each ad lists tasks a candidate can picture. The Severance Tax ad describes fieldwork at client sites to review documentation for incentive applications and refund claims, and preparing refund claims with Access and Excel. The Real Property Tax ad covers property inspections, analysing assessment notices and filing appeals. Every experienced-hire ad gives the share of time spent travelling, from up to 10 to 20% for entry consultants to 30 to 50% for the Principal.

**Students get their own proposition.** The intern ad has its own benefits list: a competitively paid internship, mentorship and hands-on training, and an all-expense-paid trip to the Dallas headquarters for summer interns. Its duties are specific, including drafting technical memoranda and helping multi-state companies with state and local tax controversies, such as preparing clients for discussions with auditors. It also gives an hourly range of $23.70 to $27.50 for Sacramento, CA.

**The Principal ad explains the title.** The Principal ad says Ryan is not a CPA firm and that the Principal title is similar to an equity partner in public accounting. It also says the role has no location preference. This is the one passage in the material that explains Ryan’s structure from the candidate’s side.

## The main problems

**The promise of control over your own time is contradicted in the same ad.** The employer paragraph makes flexibility a company-wide promise. The Principal ad sets a 50+ hour standard workweek and up to half the time travelling, and does not explain how the two fit. The only other content on flexibility is a three-word label. This is the one claim in the material that the material itself contradicts. It is ranked high rather than critical because the contradiction appears in one of the five ads.

> Employer paragraph in every ad: “With a clear understanding of expectations and accountabilities, our employees are given ownership of their time and flexibility to meet the demands of their professional and personal lives.”

**The employer paragraph in every ad is stock phrases ending in a superlative.** These two sentences are the only description of Ryan as an employer that appears on the job board and in every ad. Every phrase in them could be moved into a comparable firm’s copy unchanged. The closing claim compares Ryan with the whole industry and offers no basis for the comparison. The concrete benefits list sits above the paragraph, but the paragraph does not refer to it.

> “Ryan offers outstanding opportunities to work in a dynamic, rapidly expanding tax services firm serving the world’s most respected Global 5000 companies.”

**Growth is promised in three places and explained nowhere.** None of the touchpoints shows a level structure, typical time to promotion, promotion figures or an intern conversion number. The only career-level detail is in the Principal ad, which will consider candidates at Managing Director or Director level who are ready for promotion. It does not say whether such candidates come from inside Ryan. Tuition reimbursement after one year and training on the Internal Revenue Code are inputs. They do not show a path.

> Employer paragraph: an accelerated growth path for high performers.

**Recognition is claimed but not named, while specific rankings sit unused on another site.** As written in the ads, the award claims contain no award, year or criterion. The specific rankings exist on an independent page, and the parents ranks line up with the 14-week paid parental leave in the same ads. The ads do not connect them. Only the internship awards (Handshake Early Talent Award, Vault’s Top Internship Programs) are named, and they too come without a year.

> Why Ryan? list: “Award-Winning Culture”.

## Where to start

1. Decide what flexibility means for each role and state it in the ad, starting with the Principal ad.
2. Replace the shared employer paragraph with copy built from facts the ads already contain, and name the awards with list and year.
3. Publish the career path: the levels from Consultant to Principal, typical timing, and how many interns join full-time.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Colleagues and team** (9 / 38): Answers describe colleagues who share knowledge, work as a team and have long tenure.
- **Flexibility and remote work** (5 / 38): Answers mention flexible hours, taking time off when needed and working from home. One says working from home is occasional. None speaks about senior roles, so the reviews say nothing about the 50+ hour week in the Principal ad.
- **Pay and benefits** (6 / 38): Five answers name what they value in their pay and benefits, including paid sports club memberships and maternity leave, both of which match items in the ads’ benefits list, as well as commissions and bonus opportunity. One says pay felt low for the role until it was raised with HR.
- **Hiring process** (6 / 38): Answers describe meeting the direct supervisor and the department head, and recruiters who set realistic expectations. One suggests take-home tests. The ads describe no hiring steps.
- **Leadership** (5 / 38): Answers call leaders open, transparent and knowledgeable. The candidate material makes no specific promise about leadership.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

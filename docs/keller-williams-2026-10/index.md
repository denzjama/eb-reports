*The offer to agents is specific and partly backed: profit share, the annual commission cap, and keeping your own brand and database. But the clearest proof sits in a magazine article, not on the Join KW pages. Two money promises go further than the company's own fine print, and the offer to KWRI headquarters staff is generic.*

**Employer Brand Score — 65 out of 100** · coverage 100% · high reliability

- The agent offer has real substance. Profit share, the annual commission cap and keeping your brand and database depend on how the KW franchise model works. Five of the eight main promises have meaningful support, though none has strong support.
- The most candid and decision-useful text is the October 2025 Outfront article on why top agents join. It explains the cap and names real downsides. The Join KW pages repeat the promises without that detail.
- Two money promises go further than the material's own details. Profit share is sold as zero risk with unlimited earning potential. KSCORE promises zero cost, while the same material mentions partial scholarships and fees that students pay.
- The KWRI headquarters offer rests on a line about making an impact. Its development evidence is real-estate training that prepares people for an agent career, and no staff member speaks anywhere in the public sources reviewed.
- In a thin sample of 7 Payscale reviews, reviewers praise flexibility and working from home, which the benefits page promises. They criticise pay and pay transparency, which the KWRI pages never mention.

## What works

**Profit share is explained as a mechanism you can check.** The Profit Share page explains where the money comes from. Associates who have not yet met their cap pay company dollar to the market center. The market center covers its monthly operating expenses, and part of the remaining profit goes to associates who brought productive agents in. A sponsor gets 50% of the profit attributed to the associates they bring on, falling across seven levels but never below 5%. The page reports over $2B distributed to KW associates. Few promises in the material are this specific.

**The Outfront article answers the questions an experienced agent would ask.** The October 2025 article explains the cap: the most an agent pays their market center from commission in a year, counted from the agent's anniversary date, after which the agent keeps 100 percent of commission for the rest of that year. It says market-center owners set splits locally. It says agents keep their own brand and can take their business with them if they leave. It also names downsides: the range of resources can feel like drinking from a firehose, moving to a market center of 200 people or more can be an adjustment, and some agents stay away because they will not be number one.

**KSCORE shows the path to a licence step by step.** The May 2025 KSCORE article sets out how to enrol through a participating market center and what a full scholarship covers. It says students pay licence application and exam costs, which vary by state, and that coursework must be finished within six months of enrolment. It explains that market centers may onboard differently. These are the conditions a person paying for their own licence needs to plan.

**KWRI benefits and hiring steps are concrete.** The benefits page states up to eight weeks of paid parental leave for eligible team members after birth, adoption or foster placement. It lists medical, dental, vision and pet insurance, flexible start and end times, and an Austin headquarters gym with a sauna and cold plunge. The careers page lists five hiring steps, from application to reference checks and an offer from an HR representative.

## The main problems

**The KWRI headquarters offer is generic, and its growth promise has no headquarters career evidence behind it.** The headline line contains nothing specific to KWRI. The growth support is training for a different career, as an agent. It says nothing about moving up inside headquarters teams. Pay, career paths and staff accounts are all missing, so a headquarters candidate gets benefits and a hiring process but no reason to choose KWRI over a comparable employer.

> “Working at KWRI is about more than just “having a job” — it’s about making an impact.” (KWRI Careers)

**The KSCORE zero-cost promise goes further than its own conditions.** The headline promises zero cost. The material's own details mention partial scholarships, state exam and application fees paid by the student, an Iowa limit, and enrolment through participating market centers. The conditions are real and useful, but they sit in an article and in fine print, away from the promise.

> “With zero costs to you and self-paced instruction, the prelicensing course, provided by Kaplan, can be completed at your own speed and adapted to fit your schedule.” (KSCORE home)

**Profit share, the most distinctive promise, is sold with absolutes and explained with a copy error.** Zero risk and unlimited earning potential are absolutes that nothing on the page supports. The over $2B figure is a total distributed to associates, with no time frame on the page and no indication of what a typical associate receives. The page also claims the percentages beat other wealth-building systems in the industry, with no source. The duplicated section, the unexplained vesting condition and the line about not shaving cash off each deal, set next to company dollar paid on each deal, make the mechanism harder to follow.

> “Profit Share is a program for U.S. and Canadian associates with zero risk and unlimited earning potential that rewards you simply for helping the KW franchise system grow.” (Profit Share page)

**The money side of the agent role is left to the conversation with a market center.** Economics come first in the article's own advice on choosing a company, yet the candidate-facing pages give principles without figures. Next to the zero-risk wording on the Profit Share page, the absence of any financial downside leaves the offer uniformly positive.

> The Outfront article says market-center owners decide the commission split and that the cap is predetermined, but gives no amount or range.

## Where to start

1. Rewrite the Profit Share page: remove the absolutes, fix the duplicated section, explain vesting, and bring the cap and portability facts onto Join KW.
2. Put the KSCORE cost conditions right next to the zero-cost headline.
3. Build the KWRI staff offer from evidence: staff stories, internal career paths, and pay ranges in job ads.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy.

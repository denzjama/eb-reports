*The job ads are concrete and candid about hard conditions. The careers page above them leans on stock phrases, and restaurant roles, which it lists first, get the fewest facts.*

**Employer Brand Score — 66 out of 100** · coverage 96% · medium reliability

- The job ads do the heavy lifting. Five of the seven publish a pay range and an exact schedule, and they name hard conditions plainly, from cold rooms and heavy lifting to graveyard shifts and the planned closure of the Irvine office.
- The careers landing page, where candidates start, rests on stock phrases: a great place to work and grow, great benefits, competitive wages, family. Its four restaurant highlights are generic and come with no detail.
- Restaurant candidates get the fewest facts. The store ad gives no pay figure, “Flexible scheduling” is never explained, and the unfiltered list of 65 open roles on the capture date contained no restaurant roles.
- The most ownable promise is buried. One store ad states that all Store Managers and Divisional Managers started as hourly Associates and that everyone starts as a Level 1 Store Associate. This appears nowhere on the landing page, which offers only the words “Career Growth”.
- Among 58 Comparably answers, team and coworkers draw mostly positive comments, while pay and management draw mixed ones. Fairness and appearance rules come up only in reviews.

## What works

**Pay ranges and exact schedules in most ads.** Five of seven ads publish a range: $23.95 to $25.50 an hour for the Steam Cleaner, $22.75 to $24.50 for the Meat Department General Helper, $23.27 to $25.74 for the Inventory Clerk, $127,000 to $150,000 plus bonus for the FSQA manager and $107,000 to $140,000 plus bonus for the QA engineer. The same ads give exact hours, such as 11:00 pm to 9:30 am, four nights a week, Tuesday to Friday, for the Steam Cleaner.

**Hard conditions are stated, not hidden.** The ads name lifting limits of 50 to 75 lbs, work in temperatures as low as 34°F, weekend and holiday availability, 20 to 50% travel for the FSQA role and overnight hotel stays for the Steam Cleaner. Some of these come with how they are handled, for example a company van and weekends off for the night cleaning shift.

**Early warning about the headquarters move.** The QA engineer ad explains that the Irvine office will close in 2029, that new hires there can join the group move to Tennessee with relocation assistance or work from Baldwin Park, and says the news is shared early so candidates can make informed decisions. It answers a question a candidate would otherwise discover after joining.

**A concrete entry and promotion path for restaurant roles.** The store ad says everyone starts as a Level 1 Store Associate, prior work experience is not required, and all Store Managers and Divisional Managers started as hourly Associates and were promoted with the help of an ongoing training and development program. It is the most specific employer promise in the material.

## The main problems

**The careers landing page makes its case with stock phrases, while the specific facts sit in the ads.** These lines would fit any comparable employer unchanged. The page contains no figure, practice or example, though the ads have many. A reader of the landing page alone learns little that is particular to In-N-Out.

> “Since 1948, In-N-Out has been a great place to work and grow.”

**Restaurant candidates get the fewest facts about pay and hours.** The landing page lists restaurant employment first, yet this is the only group with no pay figure and no schedule. Production roles at the same company get both. The flexible schedule is a promised benefit that the material never describes.

> The store ad lists “Competitive hourly wage with regular opportunity for advancement and raises” and “Flexible scheduling” with no figure and no description of how shifts work.

**“Higher starting wages” is a comparison with nothing behind it.** Higher than whom is not stated, and no starting wage for restaurant roles appears anywhere in the material read. The same page then uses the weaker word competitive for the same group, which blurs the promise.

> “We’re committed to offering higher starting wages, great benefits and opportunities to grow.”

**Growth is promised to everyone but shown only for restaurant roles.** For support, production and specialist roles the growth promise has no path, programme or example in the material. Even for restaurants, the training and development program is named but not described.

> Landing page: “Career Growth” for restaurant and Cookout Truck, and “outstanding career opportunities” for support departments.

## Where to start

1. Publish restaurant starting pay and explain what “flexible schedule” means in practice.
2. Move the promotion-from-within fact and the Level 1 entry point to the top of the landing page, and show what growth looks like outside the restaurants.
3. Add accounts from real Associates, starting with managers who began as hourly Associates.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Team and coworkers** (18 / 58): Most mentions describe friendly, supportive colleagues and friendships. Four answers describe poor communication or complaining among coworkers, one saying people often go home upset.
- **Management and HR** (13 / 58): Praise for managers who help and train. Criticism of HR showing little care, managers not following through, and one account of being left off the schedule and unpaid for two months.
- **Pay and benefits** (11 / 58): Five answers praise medical benefits, schooling or earnings. Six ask for more pay or say they get no benefits beyond the shift meal.
- **Workload and pace** (5 / 58): Answers mention labour-intensive shifts, short breaks, long days and tiredness. The store ad names the pace but says nothing about breaks or shift length.
- **Fairness and appearance rules** (4 / 58): Answers mention unequal treatment by a manager and rules on covering tattoos and hair length. None of the pages read mention appearance standards.

*A recognisable, principle-led employer whose lead promise, grow by moving across roles, businesses and countries, reaches candidates with no evidence that such moves happen, while its one Mars-only idea, pets at work, is filed as a perk.*

**Employer Brand Score — 62 out of 100** · coverage 91% · high reliability

- The only promise that depends on Mars itself is Dogs @ Work, which the home page ties to the petcare purpose with four employee quotes from four cities. The benefits page lists it under perks. The lead promises of growth, care and impact contain no Mars-specific detail and could be published unchanged by any large group.
- The lead promise is growth through a new role, a new area of the business or a new location. No figure, no readable story and no example of a move sits beside it. The learning offers, such as Mars U, coaching and LinkedIn Learning, are listed on another page and never linked to moving.
- The strongest proof is regional. The Australia and New Zealand inclusion page shows dated 2026 activity, 40+ trained mental-health confidants, 16 weeks of fully paid parental leave and named recognitions. The clearest hiring FAQs are in the Australia and Middle East sections. The global pages reviewed carry mostly lists and adjectives.
- Factory and operations candidates get the least. The Supply Chain & Engineering page counts 20,000 Associates but says nothing about shifts, pay logic, safety or training. The only factory-floor employee content found is four Hungarian-language shorts on the corporate YouTube channel.
- In 63 review texts, culture, team and management are mixed, and pay is mostly positive. Workload and production conditions are mostly negative. The careers pages reviewed say least about workload and production conditions.

## What works

**Pets at work is the one idea only Mars can claim.** The home page ties dogs at work to the purpose “A BETTER WORLD FOR PETS”. It gives a ten-year timeframe and four quotes from employees named by first name in London, Washington D.C., Mexico City and Tokyo, each describing a concrete effect, such as a dog serving as a finance team mascot. It is the only promise in the material that would stop making sense if another employer's name replaced Mars.

**The hiring FAQs answer practical questions.** The Australian and Middle East pages say the application takes about 25 minutes and list what is needed. They say who sees the data, that several roles can be applied for, how to ask for adjustments, and that employment gaps need not be explained. A fraud notice says candidates are never asked for fees or financial details. The Australian inclusion page names a contact person for transgender applicants.

**The Five Principles are carried into hiring.** Two named Talent Acquisition leaders advise candidates to study the Five Principles and be ready to discuss how they match their own values. The Make the Difference Awards, held every other year, recognise Associates who put the principles into action. The culture promise therefore has a practice behind it, not only a list of values.

**The inclusion page shows practice with dates.** The Australia and New Zealand page lists six local resource-group chapters, over 40 Hear2Help Ambassadors trained as Mental Health First Aiders, 16 weeks of fully paid parental leave for all parents regardless of gender or sexual orientation, a First Nations work-experience programme at the Wodonga site, and dated 2026 activities and recognitions.

## The main problems

**The lead promise, growth by moving across roles, businesses and countries, has no evidence that such moves happen.** This is the promise the home page leads with, and the material gives a candidate nothing to check it against. The learning offers are inputs, they sit on another page, and nothing connects them to moving. The site holds 129 story pages, but none of their text could be read, so any proof they contain was not visible in this audit.

> Home page: “At Mars, career development is shaped by opportunity, mobility, and possibility. Associates are empowered to grow, whether it’s with a new role, a new area of our global business, or a new location.”

**Factory and operations candidates get almost nothing specific.** The site map lists production, packaging and material-handling operator roles among the job titles. The careers pages reviewed give these candidates no work reality and no decision facts. Factory-floor employee content exists, but only in one language on a corporate channel. In the reviews, workload and production conditions are the most frequent negative theme. The careers pages reviewed do not address it.

> The Supply Chain & Engineering page says 20,000 Associates work in the function and that they could be expected to take personal ownership for managing equipment design, logistics, workforce deployment, management and more. It says nothing about shifts, pay logic, safety, training or progression.

**Superlatives stand in for facts on the benefits and home pages.** None of these comparisons has a source or a basis in the material. The parental-leave line already has a precise US fact, and the superlative adds a market claim the page does not support. A precise fact stated plainly makes the case without it.

> “Market-leading parental leave (e.g., 18 weeks fully paid for both parents in the US)”

**The flexibility promise has no stated boundaries.** The promise speaks to everyone, and the qualifier sits two sections away without saying which roles or sites it covers. A factory or retail candidate cannot tell from the material whether any of the choice applies to them.

> Benefits page: “With a workforce spanning the globe, we’ve already been supporting flexible working for more than a decade. And now we’re able to offer even more freedom – with even greater choice about where, when and how you work.”

## Where to start

1. Put evidence of internal moves (yearly figures and three or four story bodies) next to the growth promise on the home page.
2. Build a factory and operations page covering shifts, pay logic, safety and training, with operator voices, and subtitle and link the existing factory shorts.
3. Replace the superlatives and the open-ended flexibility line with scoped facts, and give the volunteering figures a current year.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Culture and team** (24 / 63): Supportive, collaborative colleagues appear often. One author says the Five Principles are taken seriously and have consequences. Others describe gossip, a two-faced culture, and poor performers moved around the organisation. The family framing is praised by one author and criticised by another.
- **Management and leadership** (17 / 63): Praise for approachable, engaged leaders sits beside requests for better communication, fairness on promotions, change management and understanding of the workforce.
- **Pay and benefits** (10 / 63): Seven of the ten are positive about base pay, bonus, pension or medical benefits. One says salary is no longer competitive, one says pay should rise, and one calls the benefits programme inconsistently applied.
- **The hiring process** (9 / 63): Authors describe interviews as relaxed, informative and clear about expectations. Criticism covers slow replies and hiring too broadly.
- **Workload, schedule and conditions** (7 / 63): Most describe production conditions: dress and safety rules, an attendance point system, short-staffed shifts, pressure to work long hours, little for the night shift. One shift worker rates the hours as good.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

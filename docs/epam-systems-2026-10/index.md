*EPAM’s job ads show the work in concrete technical detail, but the reasons to choose EPAM appear in one of ten ads, and there they rest on stock phrases, superlatives and client counts.*

**Employer Brand Score — 59 out of 100** · coverage 91% · medium reliability

- The ads show the work: seven of ten describe concrete systems, tools and hand-offs, and four put AI tools into the daily duties.
- Only one of ten ads says what EPAM offers in return. Four show neither a benefits section nor a benefits selector; five have a location selector whose contents did not load in the capture.
- Where promises appear, they use stock phrases, superlatives and client counts. The most specific material (AI-native delivery pods, spec-driven development, named tools) sits in the duties and is never used as a reason to join.
- On YouTube, 5 of the 15 latest videos are about people or hiring. They lean on awards and culture adjectives; the one employee story is told in the company’s words.
- Reviews (not scored): colleagues and learning are mostly positive; pay, management and the offer stage of hiring are mixed. One answer describes a level-up path with a competency matrix that no employer page reviewed mentions.

## What works

**The ads show the actual work.** Seven of the ten ads describe concrete duties, systems and tools. The network ad names the Cisco ACI fabric, F5 BIG-IP migrations and Terraform and Ansible pipelines. The full-stack ad names FastAPI, Pandas, Polars, DuckDB and Azure Container Apps. The delivery-manager ad names the metrics the role tracks: throughput, lead time, quality gates.

**AI work appears as duties.** Four ads put AI tools into the daily work: driving spec-driven development and AI-assisted workflows, pairing with GitHub Copilot on vulnerability triage, building agentic systems with evals and observability, and using GitHub Copilot and Claude in spec-driven workflows. Two more ask for AI familiarity. This is the best-supported claim in the material, and the support sits in the duties.

**Some ads name the hard parts.** The security ad says the vulnerabilities are mainly in legacy applications, asks for comfort working against a backlog under time pressure, and explains how the work runs: pairing with Copilot, re-scanning until findings close, feeding root causes back to the lead. The network ad mentions aggressive CVE patch timelines; the SAP ad asks for flexibility to travel.

**Basic conditions and two ways in.** Every ad states work mode and country, and nine of ten state the English level. Next to the apply button, each ad offers a second route for candidates still looking: send a CV to receive a personalized offer.

## The main problems

**Nine of ten job ads give no reason to choose EPAM in the captured text.** A reader of the security, network, sales or SAP ad learns what the work is and nothing about what EPAM offers in return. The promises made in the one benefits block and in the YouTube people posts do not reach the other ads.

> One ad, Principal Forward Deployed Engineer, has a benefits section. Five ads show a “Location-specific conditions & benefits” selector whose contents did not load. The security, network, sales and SAP ads show neither.

**The promises use stock phrases while the specifics sit unused in the duties.** None of the six main claims tested is specific to EPAM. The ads already contain what would make them specific: AI-native delivery pods, spec-driven development, named tools, legacy remediation with Copilot. That material is never offered as a reason to join.

> “Advanced Tech Stack: Work on innovative projects and apply or enhance your expertise in Cloud, Data, AI and other emerging technologies.” (Lithuania-relocation ad)

**Client and footprint figures say nothing about the role.** The figures are specific, but the ad does not say which client or industry the engineer would work for, and its duties and requirements do not mention life sciences. “Work closely with 340+” describes the company’s client base, not the role.

> “World-Class Clients: Work closely with 340+ of the Forbes Global 2000 on creating disruptive solutions that make a global impact”

**The growth promise lists inputs, not a path.** No levels, no description of how someone moves up, no outcome figures. The list appears in one ad tied to Lithuania, and the material does not say whether it holds in other hiring countries.

> “Professional Growth: Exceptional support for career development with comprehensive resources for upskilling or reskilling in pioneering practices”

## Where to start

1. Add a short, role-specific “why EPAM” block to every ad, starting with the four that have none.
2. Rewrite the benefit pillars from the specifics already in the ads, and drop the superlatives.
3. Publish what happens after someone applies and how progression works.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Colleagues and culture** (11 / 56): Answers describe helpful, smart colleagues and openness. One critical answer describes too much freedom not to follow processes.
- **Learning and growth** (7 / 56): One answer says learning is encouraged but no time is set aside for it. Another describes a level-up path with a competency matrix, which the employer material reviewed does not mention.
- **Pay and benefits** (9 / 56): Positive answers mention pay on schedule, bonus and health care. Critical ones mention pressure not to take leave, a medical certificate for one day of sick leave, and long-term incentives only at senior levels.
- **Hiring process** (9 / 56): Positive answers describe skills-based interviews and fair follow-up. Critical ones ask for more detailed feedback and describe long evaluations followed by a low offer; three of these use very similar wording.
- **Management** (8 / 56): Some answers praise leaders’ engineering mindset and accountability. Others ask leaders to listen more and be more human-centric, and describe ineffective managers in some locations.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

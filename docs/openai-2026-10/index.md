*The job ads show concrete work that only OpenAI could describe. The reason to join is the company mission, repeated word for word in every ad, and most promises around it rest on self-description.*

**Employer Brand Score — 66 out of 100** · coverage 87% · medium reliability

- The job ads describe the work concretely: named tasks, tools, partner teams, office days and relocation. They tie it to products and models that belong to OpenAI, such as o1, o3, ChatGPT and the OpenAI API.
- The pitch for joining is the same “About OpenAI” block in all five ads: a mission statement ending with “Join us in shaping the future of technology.” It says what the company is for. It does not say what an employee gets.
- The proof and the pitch never meet. The named models sit in team introductions, while the closing block every candidate reads contains no specific fact. Two of seven promises have meaningful support, and both rest on the research-side ads.
- The one open official social channel posted 15 videos in about 18 hours, all about customers and products. None shows people at OpenAI or points to jobs.
- Reviews are too thin to weigh. Only Comparably could be read (9 reviews stated, 4 short answers visible). The answers are positive on pace, leadership and pay, and interview respondents call the process very difficult.

## What works

**The work is described concretely.** Every ad lists tasks a person could picture doing. The early-career ad names the stack it asks for (JavaScript, React, Postgres or MySQL) and says Python is used on the backend. The Account Director ad lists the stages of a deal it owns, from opportunity development to close, and a consumption revenue target. Four of the five ads state the location, three office days a week and relocation assistance.

**Specifics that belong only to OpenAI.** The RL/Reasoning ad credits its team with o1 and o3. The Applied ad has engineers own ChatGPT and OpenAI API features end to end. The Human Data ad runs from internal research tooling to production ChatGPT. These details make the frontier-work promise the one claim that a comparable employer could not reuse.

**Demands are stated, not hidden.** The early-career ad asks for the “Ability to move fast in an environment where things are sometimes loosely defined and may have competing priorities or deadlines”. The sales ad states a consumption revenue target and demand creation where the customer has not yet defined a project or budget. These lines let a reader rule themselves out, which is why the trade-off criterion scores full marks.

**Barriers lowered where it matters.** The early-career ad says interest in AI/ML is enough and direct experience is not required, and accepts equivalent practical experience in place of a degree. The sales ad considers remote applications from within the US.

## The main problems

**The reason to join is the company mission, not an offer to the employee.** The block is the only part every candidate reads in every ad, and it describes what OpenAI is for. It names no condition of work, no benefit and no fact about life inside the company. Only the Human Data ad links its team to the mission; in the other four the mission and the role sit side by side without a connection.

> All five ads close with the same “About OpenAI” block, which begins: “OpenAI is an AI research and deployment company dedicated to ensuring that general-purpose artificial intelligence benefits all of humanity.”

**Most promises rest on self-description; the proof that exists is confined to two research-side ads.** Of seven promises, two are partly supported and five are weakly supported or not supported at all. The “world’s best” comparison has nothing behind it in the material. The safety promise is made to every candidate, including sales and support, but only two research-side ads show what it means in daily work.

> “This long-term undertaking brings the world’s best scientists, engineers, and business professionals together in one lab.” (Account Director, Cyber)

**Ownership, impact and pace are claimed in the same words across ads, with no example.** These lines contain no employer-specific information, so a comparable employer could reuse them unchanged. The ads never show one thing someone on the team owned and what changed as a result, so the claim cannot be checked.

> “We’re looking for people who are excited by technical ownership, enjoy working across the stack, and are eager to solve ambiguous problems in a high-impact, fast-paced environment.” (Human Data)

**The early-career ad gives a new graduate the same pitch as a senior hire, and leaves out basic facts.** The role content is adapted to early-career engineers, but the reason to join is the same closing block used for the 14-year sales role. The ad does not answer where the job is, when it starts or what help a new engineer gets. A candidate reading it may reasonably ask all three.

> The ad asks for 0 to 1 years of experience and says direct AI/ML experience is not required.

## Where to start

1. Turn the shared “About OpenAI” block into a short offer to the employee, built on facts already in the ads.
2. Replace the repeated ownership, impact and pace lines with one real example per team.
3. Rework the early-career ad so it answers what a new graduate needs to decide: place, start, support.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

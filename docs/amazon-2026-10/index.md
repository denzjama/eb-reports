*Amazon tells hourly candidates exactly what the shifts, benefits and physical work involve, but leads with promises it does not back: unmatched scale for corporate candidates, and industry-leading wages with no pay rate for hourly ones.*

**Employer Brand Score — 71 out of 100** · coverage 100% · high reliability

- Amazon runs two employer brands. hiring.amazon.com gives hourly candidates shift windows, weekly hour bands, benefits by schedule type with their 90-day conditions, and the physical demands. amazon.jobs opens with "Impact the future, today" and a paragraph a comparable employer could publish unchanged.
- The hourly offer leads with "Industry-leading wages", yet none of the 7 hourly pages captured gives a pay rate. The only hourly pay figure in the material is a related-article headline on the Amazon newsroom.
- Amazon's most ownable idea is Career Choice and the promise that you can build a future at Amazon or somewhere else. It is the only core promise that is fully specific to Amazon and it appears on three pages, but it leads neither site, and its participation figure appears as 200,000, more than 250,000 and more than 300,000.
- The Leadership Principles are the most specific asset for corporate roles: they state hard expectations openly and show up in interview preparation and job duties. Growth for corporate employees has no named mechanism or figure behind it.
- No employee reviews could be read, so the perception layer is empty. On YouTube, 13 of the 15 latest videos were uploaded within four days in October 2023.

## What works

**Hourly benefits are spelled out by schedule, with their conditions.** The hourly benefits page splits benefits by regular schedules of 30 to 40, 20 to 29 and 0 to 19 hours a week, plus seasonal schedules. It says which items start after 90 days of employment and that part-time dental and vision cover only the employee. A candidate can see what each schedule includes before choosing it.

**Shift times and weekly hours are given as numbers.** Shifts and Schedules gives start and end windows: early morning shifts typically start between 4:00 and 6:00 a.m. and end between 2:00 and 4:00 p.m.; evening and overnight shifts typically start between 10:00 p.m. and 4:00 a.m. Hour bands are 40, 30 to 39, 20 to 29, and a minimum of 4 for flex time. The page also states the limits: shifts vary by job type and part-time hours depend on business needs.

**The hard parts of hourly work are stated.** The fulfilment centre page lists lifting up to 49 pounds, a fast-paced warehouse and facilities kept between 60°F and 90°F. YouTube role descriptions mention cold refrigerated areas with gear provided and heights up to 35 feet. The hiring-process page says plainly: "You don’t get paid to go to a pre-hire appointment — it is part of the application process."

**Every US corporate ad captured states a salary range.** All five US job ads give a base salary range tied to the location, for example 70,000 to 90,900 USD a year for the Transportation Area Manager in Byhalia, MS, and say the package includes sign-on payments and restricted stock units. The Water Infrastructure Engineer ad also encourages candidates to apply even if they do not meet all the preferred qualifications.

## The main problems

**The hourly pay promise is a superlative with no pay rate behind it.** Pay is the first item of the hourly offer, and none of the 7 hourly pages captured states a rate. Nothing in the material supports the comparison in industry-leading. The pay figure that does exist sits on a different site, covers full-time core operations employees only, and has no date in the captured text.

> Why Choose Amazon leads its benefits section with "Industry-leading wages", followed only by weekly pay, overtime above 40 hours and time-and-a-half for holidays.

**The amazon.jobs lead promise could belong to any large employer, and nothing on the site backs it.** The paragraph is built from stock phrases (impact, endless opportunities) and a superlative (unmatched scale). Its only specific content is the list of things customers do, which describes Amazon's businesses, not the work. No page captured shows what endless opportunities means for an employee, and the Our Workplace page that follows does not return to the impact idea.

> "Every Amazonian, from warehouse floor to corporate HQ, shares a drive to impact the future, today. Our unmatched scale offers every employee endless opportunities to reimagine how customers shop, stream, eat, connect and live. United by our peculiar culture of curiosity, bold ownership and fearless initiative, we channel our customer obsession into making everyday impact—big and small."

**Growth for corporate roles is asserted without a mechanism; the growth proof shown belongs to hourly staff.** No programme, path or figure is given for corporate employees. Whether corporate employees can use Career Choice is not stated, so the growth proof in the material cannot be read as support for corporate growth.

> "Amazon employees are encouraged to enter different roles, take on new challenges, and learn new skills. Our internal transfer opportunities and career development programs help employees build their careers." (Benefits and Stock)

**Career Choice participation is given as three different numbers.** This is the one core promise that is fully specific to Amazon, and its headline figure changes between pages and within one article. The material does not say which figure is current or whether 200,000 counts the US only.

> The hourly career advancement page shows 200,000 participants and counting, with no date or scope.

## Where to start

1. Publish hourly starting pay by role and location where "Industry-leading wages" stands now.
2. Rebuild the amazon.jobs lead around the Leadership Principles and give corporate growth a named mechanism and figures.
3. Settle on one dated Career Choice figure and make building a future at Amazon or somewhere else the headline of hourly growth.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

*The deep pages carry real substance: a level-by-level consulting ladder, a detailed London hiring FAQ, job ads with tasks and US pay. The front pages lead with lines any firm could publish, and the hardest demands of the job appear only in the internship posting's fine print.*

**Employer Brand Score — 66 out of 100** · coverage 100% · high reliability

- The front pages lead with lines that fit any comparable firm: the rotating Be more headline, problems that matter, and it's the people. The material that only Bain could publish sits two or three clicks deeper: the eight-level consulting ladder, the home office model and the London office's extra 10% pro-bono projects.
- Five of eight promises are partly supported and none is fully supported. Apprenticeship and mentoring is the best backed, through the consulting ladder. The most repeated proof point, the Glassdoor ranking in 7 of 8 job ads, gives no year, list scope or criteria in the ads.
- The plainest statement of what consulting demands is in the internship posting's essential functions: an unpredictable schedule, regular travel and being in person most days. The consulting page instead lists Enjoy a sustainable career and presents travel as a matter of preference. On Comparably, 2 of 38 answers raise workload.
- Practical information is strong for London graduates and US roles, with dates, assessment content, an AI-use rule and pay ranges. Six of eight job ads, all outside the US, give no pay information, and on the capture date the London page still said applications were open, twelve days after the stated deadline.
- On YouTube, 2 of the 15 latest videos are about working at Bain. The channel functions as a client-insight channel, and none of the videos links to jobs.

## What works

**The consulting ladder shows how people grow.** The Management Consulting page describes eight levels from intern to partner and what changes at each: senior associate consultants are asked to mentor associate consultants, managers select their own case team, and associate partners apprentice directly under a partner. This turns the mentoring promise into a visible mechanism.

**The London graduate page answers practical questions.** It gives the closing date, the assessment content (numerical and logical reasoning plus a personality questionnaire), two case-interview rounds with their timing, start months, no cover letter for London, how international grades are read, and a named rule on AI: only for grammar and spelling in the CV, not in the assessment or interviews.

**Job ads show the work and, in the US, the pay.** The Chicago HR coordinator ad names the cohort supported (first-year associate consultants), the systems (Workday), the Illinois salary range of $58,000 to $69,750 and in-office presence at least 3 days per week. The AI engineering lead ad describes three partner teams and what the role decides before anything ships.

**The technology offer has its own proof.** The Technology & Engineering page names client builds with figures, for example an AI assistant deployed to more than 6,000 employees across more than 2,300 stores. The AI engineering lead ad describes a standing product team inside the firm. This is the most concretely adapted segment in the sample.

## The main problems

**The front pages lead with claims any firm could make, and the specific material sits deeper.** Each of these lines stays true if another firm's name is put in. The comparative claims (the best in the field, faster than almost anywhere else) have no comparison behind them. Meanwhile the facts that only Bain can state (the eight-level ladder, the home office model of bigger, fewer hubs, one week of training before an intern joins a case team) appear on the Management Consulting, Hybrid and Internship pages, not where the offer is made.

> Careers home: "Work alongside the best in the field, on problems that matter, and build skills that compound faster than almost anywhere else."

**What consulting demands is stated only in the internship posting's fine print.** The material already contains an honest description of the job's demands, but it sits in a compliance block of one programme page. The proposition pages present travel as a matter of interest and promise sustainability without saying what makes the career sustainable. The material does not say whether the internship's schedule and travel terms also describe associate consultant and consultant roles. On Comparably, 2 of 38 answers raise workload.

> Associate Consultant Internship, essential functions: "Given the nature of client service work, consultants are regularly required to work an unpredictable schedule to meet the performance expectations and deadlines essential to this role."

**The office-attendance promise is a principle with no rule, while some ads set fixed minimums.** The hub page gives no number, office or role, so the principle cannot be checked. Where ads do give a rule it is precise, which shows the information exists. For five of the eight sampled roles a candidate cannot tell how many days are expected in the office.

> Hybrid and office work: "Our guiding principles ensure you'll always know what's best for you, the client, and Bain, and as a result, whether to work remotely or in-person."

**The interview-feedback promise has no mechanism behind it.** This is a specific, checkable promise. None of the hiring-process pages, the London page or the internship page says who gives feedback, when, or in what form, and the scoring system is not described.

> Our Hiring Process: "After each interview, you’ll learn exactly how it went."

## Where to start

1. Rebuild the careers home and Life at Bain around the apprenticeship model and its proof, and cut or back the superlatives.
2. Put the demands of consulting next to the sustainability promise, and publish the office-attendance rule for each role.
3. Make the hiring promises checkable and current: explain how post-interview feedback works and refresh the London page.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Colleagues, collaboration and coaching** (10 / 38): Answers praise helpful colleagues and collaboration; one describes coaching across the ranks.
- **Leadership and managers** (7 / 38): Six answers are positive about care and openness; one asks for more trust in leadership.
- **Pay and benefits** (7 / 38): Five answers praise pay, health insurance or the performance bonus. The two answers under feeling undervalued point to workload and team culture rather than pay.
- **Workload** (2 / 38): One answer says the volume of work and expectations is a lot; another asks for more hiring to balance work.
- **Flexibility and life outside work** (2 / 38): One answer mentions working from home three days a week. Role and office are unknown, so it cannot be compared with the in-office rules in the job ads.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

*Meta’s careers site leads with AI and with products, but its offer to employees is one generic sentence, and the concrete proof sits in two leadership blog posts, away from the promises it could support.*

**Employer Brand Score — 55 out of 100** · coverage 92% · high reliability

- The home page opens with AI, then spends most of its length on 14 product and app descriptions written for users of those products. The offer to employees is one sentence about impact and strong teams.
- Two of eight promises have meaningful support, both only partly: work on AI, and cross-team collaboration. That support sits in two blog posts by a vice president and a senior director, on separate pages from the promises.
- Several main lines rest on superlatives with no figure or comparison behind them: building and learning faster than anyone else, the greatest compute per AI researcher, the brightest minds in tech.
- The material already holds two answers it does not use. The culture page names demanding conditions (high performance, an in-person focus, hard conversations) that could become an honest offer if explained. Ming Hua’s story shows how the values Move fast and Focus on long-term impact fit together, through separate teams for different time horizons.
- The outside view is missing. No official careers social account could be confirmed from the careers site, and every review site blocked automated reading, so this audit says nothing yet about how Meta is seen by employees.

## What works

**Two stories show real technical work.** Omar’s story names the actual problems of his team: light takes 5 microseconds to travel a kilometre, and a failure has to be traced to one component in a 100K+ GPU data center with millions of components. It also names a working practice: one team both builds and operates the network. Ming Hua describes starting in wearables with hardware and embedded systems new to her, working first on WhatsApp for wearables, then on problems such as Bluetooth connectivity and battery life. This is the most concrete material on the site.

**A career path with dates and a method.** Ming Hua joined Meta in 2009 as engineering director for local and business platforms and is now Vice President of Wearables Devices. The story names her method for entering a new field, a small, deep “minimally viable ramp-up project”, and admits that diving into a new area is hard. Omar has been with Meta since 2013 and has worked on nearly every part of its network. Both accounts are about people who are now senior leaders.

**The culture page states demanding expectations.** The page describes a culture of high impact, high performance and high reward, a value about being direct and willing to have hard conversations, and an in-person focus with a selective approach to remote work. These statements let a candidate judge fit, even though none of them is yet explained in practical terms.

**A short route to jobs and practical safeguards.** Each captured page leads to the job search, which can be filtered by technology, team, location or reference code. The home page links to a warning about recruiting scams, and the accessibility page links to an accommodation request form.

## The main problems

**The careers home page is mostly product marketing, and the offer to employees is one generic sentence.** The “you” in this copy is a buyer of the product. The page’s length goes to product claims, and the only sentence about working at Meta is built from stock phrases. A candidate gets product news but no account of what the job offers.

> After the opening, the captured page carries eight product blocks (Meta Model API, Muse Image, Muse Spark, Meta Quest, Meta Glasses, Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta, Meta Ray-Ban Display and Meta Neural Band) and six app descriptions (Meta AI, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, WhatsApp, Messenger). None says what an employee does on the product.

**The main promises are built from stock phrases and superlatives the material does not back.** Each sentence makes a comparison with everyone else but gives no figure, source or example for it. The first three contain no information specific to Meta. The fourth is the only line that ties the offer to a real asset, and it rests on a superlative with no supporting number.

> “At Meta, we believe in impact and building a career where you’re challenged and valued in teams that are the best at what they do.” (home page)

**The concrete proof sits in two blog posts by senior leaders, away from the promises.** Only two of eight promises have meaningful support, and a reader of the main pages sees none of it. The only voices with more than one sentence are a vice president and a senior director, so the material does not show the experience of the people Meta is hiring now.

> The figures and practices in the captured material (100K+ GPU data centers, the 5-microsecond constraint, one team that builds and operates the network, teams set up for different time horizons) appear only in the Omar and Ming Hua stories.

**Demanding expectations are named but not explained.** This is the most honest material on the site and the most useful for self-selection, but it stops at the label. A candidate reading it may reasonably ask how many days in the office are expected, which roles can be remote, and what high performance means in a review. The culture page also lists Move fast and Focus on long-term impact without saying how they fit together.

> “We build the future of work that we want, with an in-person focus designed to support a strong, valuable experience for our people who work from the office, and a thoughtful and intentional approach to where we invest in remote work.” (culture page, Live in the future)

## Where to start

1. Replace the superlatives with the figures and practices already published in the two stories, and put them next to the promises.
2. Spell out what high performance and the in-person focus mean in practice.
3. Rebuild the home page around one offer to employees, with stories from the roles being hired.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

*A culture memo specific enough to tell candidates who should not apply, sitting behind a home page that says “Join our dream team”.*

**Employer Brand Score — 75 out of 100** · coverage 97% · high reliability

- The culture memo holds a proposition built on named practices: a team modelled on a professional sports team with the keeper test, a two-word vacation policy, and individual pay with a yearly choice between salary and stock options. It also says plainly who will not like working here.
- Little of this reaches the first screen. The home page offers “Join a star studded ensemble” and “Join our dream team”, and the customer service page gives no pay, schedule or location.
- Four of eight promises have meaningful support, none of them strong. Two of the five pillars rest on a Q1 2024 audience figure and a slogan, and growth and candor have no mechanism a candidate could check.
- The memo's “Take vacation.” line carries no scope. The work life page limits the no-prescribed-time-off rule to salaried employees, and two US ads give full-time hourly staff an accrual of 35 days.
- In 57 undated answers on Comparably, pay, colleagues and freedom draw mostly praise. Management draws 8 critical answers out of 12, three of them describing people treated as expendable or harmed. Reviews do not enter the score.

## What works

**The memo says who should not apply.** The culture memo ends its introduction with “But Netflix is not for everyone, so please read on.” It describes a team modelled on a professional sports team that will swap out someone it loves for a better player, says “Many people will be happier at companies that are more stable or take fewer risks.”, and warns that, depending on the role, employees may need to work on titles they perceive to be harmful. Each of these names a condition of the job that a reader can test against their own preferences.

**Culture is shown as policies and named practices.** The memo gives the vacation policy as two words and the expenses policy as five. It explains how decisions are made: an informed captain for every significant decision, farming for dissent, disagree then commit, and a reflection afterwards. The keeper test is spelled out with its two questions. Each term is explained where it first appears.

**Pay mechanics are published in detail.** The work life page says there is no raise pool, no set bands and grades, and no need to wait for an annual cycle to adjust pay. Employees choose each year between all cash, all options or a mix, and the 10-year options are fully vested and kept after leaving. The two ads with a captured pay section state a salary-only structure without bonuses and give ranges: $388,000 to $558,000 for the iOS role, varying by location, and $170,000 to $250,000 for the game producer.

**The memo answers its own hardest objections.** It admits the keeper test can sound scary and says what happens instead: regular conversations with managers, judgment on the whole record, and support through short-term bumps. It admits failures and that a few people took advantage of the culture. It says when managers must step in: unethical or harmful decisions, a crisis, or a new team member without full context.

## The main problems

**The home page and the customer service page carry slogans, not the offer.** The most specific part of the offer sits in one long document behind a menu item. The first screen gives a mission line and two slogans, which contain nothing specific to working at Netflix. The engineering page lists six engineering organisations and their groups; the customer service page names tasks and no conditions.

> Home page: “Join a star studded ensemble”, “Join our dream team” and “Ready to Jump In the Spotlight?” around a role search. None of the memo's specifics (keeper test, time-off and expenses policies, pay approach) appears on it.

**Two of the five pillars rest on audience size and a slogan, and the pay promise claims a market position the material does not measure.** Audience size is a fact about members. It shows nothing about what bold or experimental work means for an employee, and the figure dates from Q1 2024. The superlative about other entertainment companies and the top-of-market comparison have no source in the material. Next to these, the Dream Team and People over Process pillars are backed by named practices.

> Culture memo: “Netflix is programming for well over half a billion** people globally — something no other entertainment company has ever done before.” The footnote derives the figure from about 260 million households as of Q1 2024 and an assumed two people per household.

**The best-known time-off line is stated without its scope.** Read together, the three touchpoints describe two regimes: no prescribed time off for salaried staff, and an accrual of 35 days for full-time hourly staff in two US ads. The memo, which the early-career pages tell candidates to read before interviews, gives only the first and leaves out the word salaried. The material does not say which roles are hourly or whether the hourly rule applies outside the US.

> Culture memo, People over Process section: the vacation policy is given as the two words “Take vacation.”, with no group named.

**Experienced candidates are not told how hiring works or what the levels in job titles mean.** Interview steps are published only for the early-career routes. The experienced-hire route, which all eight sampled ads belong to, has no description of steps, timing or who the candidate meets. The level numbers are the only signal of seniority in the titles, and their meaning is not given.

> New grads page: interviews typically include a take-home assessment followed by two rounds. Internships page: a take-home assessment and about two to three rounds.

## Where to start

1. Put three or four of the memo's specifics, with their scope, on the home page and on every role-family page.
2. Add the salaried scope to the memo's vacation line, state the hourly rule, and publish how experienced hires are interviewed.
3. Collect attributable employee accounts and current figures for the pillars that now rest on audience size and slogans.

## What reviews say

*Reviews are individual opinions, not facts about the company; they never change the score.*

- **Pay and benefits** (7 / 57): Five answers praise salary or call pay competitive. Two are critical: one asks for better bonuses and stock grants, one says family insurance cost most of a paycheck.
- **Leadership and management** (12 / 57): Four answers praise clear communication and approachability. Eight are critical, and three of them describe people being treated as expendable, gaslit or harmed.
- **Colleagues and culture** (19 / 57): Fourteen answers praise colleagues or the culture. Five are critical, one saying the memo is used against new recruits by cliquey teams.
- **Freedom and autonomy** (4 / 57): Three answers name independence or freedom. One describes being told to stay quiet in meetings and denied the tools for the job.
- **Feedback and candor** (4 / 57): Three answers praise honest communication or recruiter feedback. One says feedback was given only when specifically requested.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).

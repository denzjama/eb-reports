*The job ads show concrete work, real conditions and real AI tasks. The careers pages lead with a product slogan and a people-first promise that fits any employer, and none of the five sampled ads outside the US states pay.*

**Employer Brand Score — 66 out of 100** · coverage 100% · high reliability

- The job ads carry the best material. They name the work, the schedule, travel, team size and the reporting line, and one ad states plainly what the role is not.
- The AI promise on the home page has proof, but on other pages: 8 of the 9 sampled ads describe AI tasks or ask for AI experience, while the home page offers only the slogan.
- The people promise is the weak core. “We put people first” and the wellbeing pillar rest on benefit names without amounts and on awards whose criteria are not given.
- Pay appears only in the US: the 4 sampled US ads state a base pay rate or range, and the 5 ads in India, Japan, Canada and the Netherlands state no pay and no benefits.
- The word “Flexible” on every sampled ad means at least two office days a week, but that definition sits only on the Life at page.

## What works

**Job ads show the work and its conditions.** The Federal SRE ad states: “The Federal SRE Team has 3 shifts that provide 24x7 production support for our Government Community Cloud infrastructure.” It adds the shift days and hours (third shift, 10-hour days, Wednesday to Saturday), shift bonuses for second and third shifts and a four-day week. The Sr Staff Software Engineer ad says: “This is not a machine learning or AI research role; the engineer does not train foundation models.” Other ads give travel (up to 30% for the Forward Deployed Solution Engineer) and team size (8–10 designers and/or managers for the design director).

**The AI promise reaches the work itself.** 8 of the 9 sampled ads describe AI tasks or ask for AI experience, in engineering, audit, finance, design, marketing, sales and site reliability. The Senior Auditor builds AI-enabled tools to automate manual audit steps and documents them for the team to reuse. The Sr Staff Software Engineer directs AI coding agents and sets how the organization does. Here the slogan on the home page has work behind it.

**Early careers answers practical questions.** Launchpad Academy is described as “Our year-long development program for new early-career hires.”, with monthly workshops, mentorship and support through the first year. The FAQ says internships are paid, that you can reapply and apply to as many positions as you like, and that interns may receive a full-time offer or a return internship, based on performance and business needs. The finance intern ad gives a base pay of $37.56 an hour.

**The hiring process is published with its rules.** How We Hire sets out three steps (application, interview, decision) and an aim to update candidates within a couple of weeks. The AI page says what candidates may use AI for, what they may not, and what happens if they break the rules. The FAQs also cover visa sponsorship and accommodations.

## The main problems

**The lead message is about the product, and the people promise beneath it could belong to any employer.** The first screen says what the company's platform does, not what working there offers. The people promise that follows contains no figure, practice or condition. The specifics that would anchor it (work personas, Launchpad Academy, the AI tasks in the ads) are on other pages.

> Home page, first screen: “Calling all AI-native, dream-chasing, future-shaping types” and “Join the team putting AI to work for people.”

**Outside the US, the sampled job ads state no pay and no benefits.** For five of the nine sampled roles the reward side of the offer is blank. The India location page lists six benefits, but the material does not say whether they apply to the roles advertised, and the ads do not carry them.

> The four US ads state pay as a guideline: $37.56 an hour for the finance intern, $94,000 to $155,100 for the Senior Auditor, $123,900 to $216,800 for the Brand Strategist and $127,600 to $216,900 for the Federal SRE. Each also lists health plans, a 401(k) with company match and other benefits.

**The wellbeing and benefits promise rests on names without content.** None of the six items states a length, an amount or who qualifies, so “generous” and “competitive” cannot be checked. Nothing else in the material backs the wellbeing pillar.

> Life at and the Ireland page carry the same six items: “Generous family leave”, “Matched donations”, “Annual learning stipends”, “Flexible PTO”, “Competitive retirement plan”, “Paid volunteer time”.

**Company success stands in for proof about working here.** These facts describe commercial success. The material does not say what they mean for someone joining, and the superlative has no stated basis.

> Life at: “We wouldn't be the fastest-growing enterprise software company in the world if we didn't dream big.” No source or measure is given.

## Where to start

1. Rebuild the “Why join” blocks on the home and Life at pages around practices you already publish: work personas, Launchpad Academy and the AI work described in the ads.
2. Add pay, or how pay is set, and the local benefits to job ads outside the US.
3. Give each benefit its content (how long, how much, who qualifies) and replace the sales-team hobby testimonials with accounts of the sales work.

## About Employer Brand Auditor

Employer Brand Auditor is a free, open-source plugin for Claude Code. It reads an employer brand the way a candidate does: what the company promises, what backs it up, and what sets it apart from other employers.

**How to install.** You need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). Run two commands in a terminal:

```
claude plugin marketplace add denzjama/employer-brand-auditor
claude plugin install employer-brand-auditor@employer-brand-auditor
```

**How to use it.** Ask Claude Code to "audit [company name]'s employer brand", or put your own material in the folder: an EVP, job ads, a careers page. For a fuller study with social channels and employee reviews, ask for a deep audit.

**What you get.** An HTML report with a 0–100 score across eight dimensions, a quotation behind every finding, the main problems, an action plan and rewritten copy. The method is explained inside the report. Code and docs: [github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor](https://github.com/denzjama/employer-brand-auditor).
